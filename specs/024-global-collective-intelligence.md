# Global Collective Intelligence Specification

## Purpose

Define the Global Collective Intelligence Engine: Tech Club's planetary-scale coordination layer where humans, communities, organizations, AI agents, research networks, and federated knowledge nodes continuously discover gaps, coordinate research, reason together, forecast opportunities, and expand public-benefit knowledge.

## Goals

- Transform the Global Knowledge Mesh into a living Collective Intelligence Network.
- Detect knowledge gaps and convert them into actionable research opportunities.
- Coordinate research across individuals, communities, universities, companies, citizen scientists, government labs, AI agents, and open science initiatives.
- Preserve human judgment, ethics, creativity, and context while using AI for search, analysis, pattern recognition, benchmarking, planning, simulation, and recommendation.
- Measure knowledge evolution, scientific progress, educational impact, innovation, and global collaboration through Semantiq and graph analytics.

## Requirements

- Collective intelligence begins from questions, not central authority.
- Knowledge remains decentralized, communities remain autonomous, and AI recommendations remain transparent.
- Reasoning supports distributed hypothesis building, argument mapping, evidence comparison, alternative perspectives, counterarguments, consensus, scientific debate, minority reports, and uncertainty mapping.
- Discovery supports knowledge gaps, research opportunities, cross-disciplinary links, emerging topics, unsolved problems, future technologies, societal challenges, educational needs, and scientific trends.
- Forecasts explain uncertainty.

## Architecture

The Collective Intelligence Engine composes Federation, Knowledge Graph, Semantiq, Agent OS, Community Engine, Research Engine, Scientific Atlas, Question Network, Workspace Runtime, Compute Engine, and Developer Platform contracts. It owns global discovery, knowledge gaps, coordination, reasoning, forecasting, analytics, research maps, innovation, education, collective memory, and collective-agent contracts.

## Risks

- Discovery can become centralized if analytics depend on a single control point.
- Forecasts can mislead if uncertainty and evidence are hidden.
- AI agents can over-coordinate research without human judgment and policy oversight.
- Global analytics can threaten privacy unless aggregation is federated and privacy-preserving.
- Collective reasoning can suppress minority reports if consensus is treated as truth.

## Testing

Future tests must cover discovery, gap detection, research coordination, forecast accuracy, collective reasoning, AI collaboration, global analytics, scalability, offline federation, performance, privacy, security, and regression behavior.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Collective Intelligence architecture documentation exists.
- Discovery, knowledge gaps, research coordination, reasoning, memory, forecasting, analytics, research map, collective agents, APIs, and decisions are documented.
- `@tech-club/collective-intelligence` exposes typed collective intelligence contracts.
- AI augments human reasoning and does not replace it.
- Knowledge remains decentralized and evidence-driven.

This specification authorizes architecture documentation and contract scaffolding for the Global Collective Intelligence Engine. Production global analytics, forecasting models, live research maps, and autonomous coordination workflows require later implementation approval.
